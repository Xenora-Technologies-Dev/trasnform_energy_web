summary: "On 14 July 2026 the International Renewable Energy Agency released Renewable Energy Statistics 2026. The dataset shows renewable electricity generation grew 9.8 percent in 2024 — faster than in the previous year — while non-renewable generation rose 1.4 percent. Renewables supplied 31.7 percent of global electricity, or 9,836 TWh. Asia produced 4,589 TWh, up 14.3 percent, with particularly strong solar and wind growth. IRENA also revised 2025 capacity figures: 693 GW of renewable capacity was added in 2025, taking installed renewables to 5.2 TW, about 49.5 percent of global power capacity. The Agency notes that meeting a 35 percent electrification ambition by 2035 would require renewables to reach roughly 78 percent of electricity generation — about two and a half times the 2024 share."

IRENA’s regional split is uneven by design of the energy transition so far. Asia accounts for the largest block of renewable generation and among the fastest growth rates. Other regions added generation more slowly; the Middle East posted the highest regional growth rate from a small base. Capacity data for 2025 show renewables still dominating new installations even when their share of annual capacity expansion dipped from the previous year.

The policy arithmetic in the release is stark: electrification of buildings, transport and industry only reduces emissions if the electricity is increasingly renewable. Moving from roughly one-third renewable generation to the high-70 percent range by 2035 is a different power system — more inverter-based resources, more transmission, more storage, and more demanding grid codes.

Those are the same themes now appearing in Indian draft connectivity standards and transmission-charge consultations. Global statistics do not substitute for local regulation, but they explain why that regulation is being rewritten.
