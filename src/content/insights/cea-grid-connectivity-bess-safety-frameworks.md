The connectivity draft is written for a grid that now has to absorb large volumes of inverter-based plant. It sets low- and high-voltage ride-through expectations, multiple-fault ride-through, reactive-current priority during disturbances, continuous operation across a wide frequency band, and rate-of-change-of-frequency withstand. Grid-forming inverters, VSC-HVDC and FACTS receive additional duties, including testing before connection.

Process matters as much as the numbers. The draft describes an eight-stage connectivity path from interconnection studies through connectivity agreements, first-time energisation, testing, trial run and commercial operation. Trial-run duration and method would differ by technology. Cyber-security obligations issued by CEA, CERT-In and related agencies would be a prerequisite for connection, not an afterthought.

The BESS audit framework is more specific about who may audit and what must be inspected: electrical, mechanical, and fire-and-safety competence, plant-operating experience, cell-to-system certification, fire detection and suppression, thermal-runaway monitoring, and a report that classifies risk and sets corrective timelines. Performance benchmarks discussed in the consultation — including retained output over the asset life, depth of discharge and round-trip efficiency — would sit beside safety, not replace it.
